## Listing photos: choosing and ordering
step: marketing
next: Listing photos: what an edit may and may not do

Lead with the one photo that sells the unit: usually the brightest living area or the view, shot wide and level. Then follow the walk-through: living, kitchen, bedrooms, bathroom, balcony or view, then building amenities and the façade last. Five to ten strong photos beat twenty weak ones; drop dark, tilted, blurry and near-duplicate shots. Landscape for portals and Facebook; square or 4:5 crops for Instagram.

A good listing photo is shot in daylight with the lights on, from a corner at chest height, with vertical lines straight. Beds made, counters cleared, toilet seat down, personal items out of frame.

## Listing photos: what an edit may and may not do
step: marketing

Fine to do freely: brighten, fix white balance, straighten verticals, sharpen, crop, blur a plate number or a face.

Fine but must be labelled "Virtually staged" on the photo: adding furniture to an empty room, removing furniture or clutter, removing cars or people, replacing a grey sky. Keep the unstaged photo in the set too.

Fine but must be labelled "AI-enhanced" on the photo: de-blurring, or rebuilding a small or low-resolution photo at higher resolution. The model invents the missing detail, so check textures, text and small objects against the original before posting.

Document scans (cleaning a phone photo of a title, ID or contract into a flat scan) are for reading only, not certified copies. Blurry text can be redrawn wrong: check names, numbers and dates against the paper.

Never: hiding cracks, stains or water damage, changing the view out of a window, making a room look larger than it is, or showing finishes the unit doesn't have. Under RA 7394 an advertisement that misleads about a product's real condition is a deceptive sales act, and a buyer who finds out at the viewing is a lost lead.

## Post design: layout and hierarchy
step: marketing
next: Post design: type, colour and spacing

One focal point per graphic: the hero photo carries it, text sits on it only where the photo is calm (sky, wall, floor) or on a solid band. Read order, largest first: the hook (what and where: "2BR with balcony, IT Park"), then the price, then three to four key facts (size, floor, furnishing, availability), then the call to action and contact. Anything beyond that goes in the caption, not on the image.

Leave generous margins (about 6–8% of the width on every side) and align everything to one edge or one centre line. Don't fill every corner: empty space is what makes a post look premium. Logo small, in one corner, the same corner on every post.

## Post design: type, colour and spacing
step: marketing

At most two typefaces, ideally one family in two weights. Keep a clear size scale: the hook about twice the size of the facts, the price bold. Never stretch, outline or rainbow text, and avoid all-caps paragraphs. Text over a photo needs real contrast: a dark scrim or solid band behind it, not a drop shadow on a busy background.

Use the brand colours for bands and accents, one accent colour per post. Peso amounts written ₱35,000/month or ₱6.2M, the same way everywhere. Tight, consistent spacing between related items and more space between groups; similar posts should share one template, so the feed looks like one brand.

## Captions for listing posts
step: marketing

First line is the hook and must work alone (it's all most people see): kind, area and the standout feature. Then the facts as short lines: price and what it includes, size, floor, furnishing, parking, when it's available, pet policy. Then one line on the area (what's walking distance). End with one call to action: "Message us to book a viewing." No ALL CAPS, at most a few emojis as bullets, and no client details. Only state what the inventory card says; never guess a spec.

## Critiquing a graphic
step: marketing

Check in this order and name the fix, not just the problem: is the focal point obvious in one second; can the price and location be read on a phone at arm's length; is the text on a calm area or band with enough contrast; are margins even and is everything aligned; how many fonts, sizes and colours (fewer is better); is anything on the image that belongs in the caption; is any edit unlabelled staging or hiding a defect.
